Idea 4 of 5

Isolating knowledge updates?

Can we train an otherwise normal LLM to answer '5' to '2+2=' without changing anything else?

Concretely: how isolated can a single counterfactual arithmetic edit be made, depending on how the edit is done, and what counts as the "anything else" that leaks (paraphrases of the prompt, neighbouring sums, downstream reasoning that depends on 2+2, unrelated behaviour)? Is near-perfect isolation only reached when the edit is a string-keyed exception rather than a change in what the model treats as true?

Background given to every method

Why it matters. Whether a targeted change can be made without collateral change is the central question for knowledge editing, unlearning and belief implantation. There is a tension: a perfectly isolated edit behaves like a backdoor (an exception keyed on one string), while an edit that the model "believes" should propagate to paraphrases and entailments, and so cannot be fully isolated.

What is known. The editing literature shows that edits succeed on the edited prompt but propagate poorly and unevenly to related facts (RippleEdits, 2307.12976; 2407.12828), that ROME/MEMIT cause collateral damage at scale (2401.07453), that plain fine-tuning with paraphrase and locality augmentation is competitive with dedicated editors (2402.11078), and it has standard metrics: reliability, generalisation, locality, portability (KnowEdit/EasyEdit, 2401.01286). Anthropic's synthetic-document fine-tuning work measures how deeply implanted beliefs are held. For arithmetic, the mechanism is partly understood: a bag of heuristics (2410.21272) and helical or Fourier number representations (2502.00873).

Gap. Editing studies use almost only entity-relation facts (CounterFact, zsRE, RippleEdits). No study was found that takes a single computed fact, one produced by a generalising mechanism rather than looked up, and maps how an edit to it leaks across editing methods, or that connects the leakage to the known arithmetic circuitry. The general answer "edits are not perfectly local" is already known; the computed-fact case is the new angle.

Scope and pitfalls. - Models: gated repos (google/gemma-*, meta-llama/*) need the HF_TOKEN environment variable to have been granted access; if a download is refused, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct work without it. - The question is ill-defined until "anything else" is defined. Should "two plus two" also change? Should 2+2+1? State up front what is meant to change and what is not; otherwise locality numbers cannot be interpreted. - Trivial-answer risk: with a loss that penalises drift from the base model, near-perfect isolation on the exact string may be easy, giving a flat "yes". The informative content is the trade-off between edit success and collateral change across methods, and whether the isolated edit is shallow (string-keyed) or deep, not the yes/no. - Thin test sets: a dozen probe prompts is not enough to claim "nothing else changed". One edit is also high variance; other target edits (another wrong answer, another sum, a non-arithmetic fact) and several seeds show whether a finding is specific to one prompt. - Format pitfalls: tokenisation of "2+2=", leading-space answer tokens, and raw completion versus chat template on an instruct model can make the unedited model itself unreliable on the test prompts; check the base model first. - ROME/MEMIT through EasyEdit may not install cleanly on recent models.
