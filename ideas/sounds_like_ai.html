Idea 5 of 5

Is there a 'sounds like AI' direction in the residual stream?

LLMs are known to have a lot of linear structure that controls their output styles and semantics. Is sounding like an AI one of those?

Concretely: a direction that separates human-written from AI-written text can already be read out of LLM activations. Is that direction causal for generation, so that adding or removing it makes the model's own output read as more or less AI-written to an independent detector while content and coherence are preserved? And is it a distinct "AI-ness" direction, or a relabelling of formality, domain, length, or the assistant persona?

Background given to every method

Why it matters. A steerable "AI-ness" direction would bear on machine-text detection and its evasion, on whether "sounding like AI" is the same thing as "being the Assistant", and on whether models represent "this text was written by a model".

What is known. The read-out half is essentially answered: human versus AI text is linearly separable in LLM activations. Quaremba et al. (2608.24780) show linear probes beat 16 detectors on 4 benchmarks and recover a shared machine-generated-text direction that generalises across settings. SV-Detect (2606.07313) extracts per-layer human-vs-AI directions and uses them for detection. Kuznetsov et al. (2503.03601) use Gemma-2-2b residual SAE features for detection, with some feature steering to interpret features. All three are about detection (judged from abstracts). Lu et al., "The Assistant Axis", find a dominant, steerable persona direction for the default Assistant identity that also exists in base models; that is persona, not surface style.

Gap. A run that stops at "a probe reaches high AUROC" is a replication. Open parts: (1) Causal: does steering or ablating the direction during generation change how AI-like the output reads to an independent detector, with content and coherence held? (2) Disentanglement: is the direction distinct from formality, fluency or low perplexity, domain, length or verbosity, and the Assistant axis? (3) Base versus instruct: does the direction exist in the pretrained base model, and is it amplified by chat tuning?

Scope and pitfalls. - Models: gated repos (google/gemma-*, meta-llama/*) need the HF_TOKEN environment variable to have been granted access; if a download is refused, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and the Qwen2.5-7B base model work without it. Gemma-2-2b has public Gemma Scope SAEs. An OpenRouter API key is available as OPENROUTER_KEY (for example for an LLM judge). - Corpus confounds: human and AI corpora differ in topic, length and formatting (HC3 human answers are Reddit-style), so a naive direction is partly "Reddit versus encyclopedic". Matched pairs (same question or same prefix) and cross-dataset, cross-generator tests are what show it is one direction about authorship. - Steering may degrade coherence before it shifts detector scores. A causal claim needs a random-direction baseline of equal norm, a confound-direction baseline such as formality, a plain prompt baseline ("write like a human"), and a comparison at matched coherence. - Detector validity: the detector must be independent of the activations the direction was fit on. Public detectors were trained on older generators and LLM judges are noisy on "sounds like AI"; use two independent measures. - A direction found by reading text may not control writing. That is a reportable negative result if the controls are in place.
