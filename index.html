The five research ideas

Each idea page shows the papers side by side with the judges' verdicts.

1

Do LLMs behave differently when the prompter is human vs another LLM?

It seems clear that LLMs can recognize when a text is from a human or an LLM. We could make a reasonable assumption that they "expect" prompts to come from humans, have human style. What happens if the prompt, controlled for content, reads very LLM? Might they behave differently?

6 papers · best: NeuriCo · Opus 5.5
2

LLMs are bad at keeping obvious secrets

LLMs are bad at keeping obvious secrets. For instance, when LLMs write fiction they foreshadow plot way too much because planning and prose generation are entangled: the model can't help but hint at what it knows is coming. The model wants to say what it's thinking, without being told it has to. Is this still true when it can rely on a plan it's conditioning on that sets up the future so it doesn't have to constantly get ready for that future on its own?

6 papers · best: Claude Code · Opus 5.5
3

Detect different lies

The literature already proposes several distinctions between types of false outputs (e.g., hallucination, confabulation, strategic deception). However, current lie-detection methods often evaluate detectors on datasets that do not cleanly separate these mechanisms, which makes it difficult to know what the detector is actually detecting. When LLMs produce false statements, how often are these due to epistemic failure versus incentive-driven misreporting?

6 papers · best: Claude Code · Opus 5.5
4

Isolating knowledge updates?

Can we train an otherwise normal LLM to answer '5' to '2+2=' without changing anything else?

6 papers · best: NeuriCo · Opus 5.5
5

Is there a 'sounds like AI' direction in the residual stream?

LLMs are known to have a lot of linear structure that controls their output styles and semantics. Is sounding like an AI one of those?

6 papers · best: Claude Code · Opus 5.5

The six methods

  • NeuriCo is our multi-stage research pipeline: literature review, planning, experiments and a separate paper-writing step. We ran it on Claude Code with Opus 5.5 and on Codex with GPT-5.6-sol and GPT-6-Astra.
  • Claude Code and Codex rows are a single agent session given the idea and told to research it and write the paper, with no time limit.

Every method received the same idea text. Papers were judged as written; nothing checked the reported numbers against the code. How the judging and ratings work →
